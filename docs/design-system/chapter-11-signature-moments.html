ॐ Chapter 11 · Addendum

Signature moments.

One unforgettable interaction per major surface — 3D, motion, ritual. Each catalogued, each earned, each disciplined. This is what turns a good store into an unforgettable one.

"The system carries consistency. The moments carry the wow. Every premium brand does both — and the second only lands because the first is disciplined."

The principle for Chapter 11
The four guardrails

What keeps it professional — not clumsy.

Break any one of these and it becomes a portfolio piece, not a store. Honor all four and it feels like Rolex, Cartier, Aesop.

01

One signature per surface

A page has one wow moment. Not three. Everything around it stays quiet so the wow lands. Scattered 3D reads as showy; earned 3D reads as premium.

02

Never blocks commerce

Add-to-Cart, checkout, and search work if the signature moment fails to load, fails to render, or the browser rejects WebGL. Commerce is the floor; the moment is the ceiling.

03

Degrades gracefully

Every moment has a static fallback. prefers-reduced-motion collapses animation. Old phones get a still image. Slow networks get a lightweight version. Nobody is left out.

04

Performance budget

LCP under 2.5s. 60fps during interaction. Signature code lazy-loaded and code-split per surface. Homepage never ships PDP viewer code. Bundle discipline is non-negotiable.

01
Homepage · Hero

The 3D breathing mandala.

The hero background isn't a flat SVG — it's a real 3D scene. Concentric rings of the mandala sit at different depths, each rotating at its own rate. Gold particles drift through the space toward the viewer. On cursor movement, the whole scene parallaxes gently.

/homepage · hero
Concept · running

What it is

Three concentric mandala rings at different Z-depths, rotating independently. Real particles in 3D space, drifting from behind toward the viewer. Camera parallaxes 4° on cursor.

Technical approach

Three.js scene, InstancedMesh for particles, orthographic camera, WebGL2.

Three.jsR3FWebGL2

Fallback

  • Reduced motion — static mandala SVG, no rotation.
  • No WebGL — the current CSS-animated version (already in place).
  • Mobile / low-end — 2D SVG with subtle CSS rotation.

Budget

~40KB (Three.js + scene, gzipped, code-split). Lazy-loaded after hero paint. 60fps target.

03
Product Detail Page · Media

True 360° product viewer.

The single most important 3D moment on the site. For high-value religious pieces — a ₹31,200 silver Sri Yantra or a ₹68,000 murti — customers deserve to see every angle. Rotate the piece with drag, zoom with pinch, see engravings and stonework in detail. Not a nice-to-have; this is table-stakes at this price point.

/collection/[slug] · gallery
Concept · running
◀ Rotate + Zoom ◇ Detail

What it is

Full WebGL 3D viewer. The actual product loaded as a GLB model, rotatable, zoomable. Real material lighting (copper reflection, silver gleam, gold overlay). Optional "Detail" mode zooms to specific features (engravings, stone settings).

Technical approach

Three.js with GLTFLoader. Each product ships a compressed GLB (~200-500KB). PBR materials for accurate metal rendering. Draco compression for geometry.

Three.jsGLTFLoaderDracoReact Three Fiber

Fallback

  • No WebGL / reduced motion — turntable of 12-24 high-res photos (photographer-produced, one shot per 15° angle).
  • Mobile without capable GPU — turntable JPEG version.
  • Slow network — static hero image with "Load 3D view" trigger.

Budget

~120KB Three.js core (code-split, PDP only). Per-product 200-500KB GLB. Streamed after initial paint.

Content requirement

Each product needs a 3D scan or model. Studios like Sirchoi or SketchAndFabric produce these for ~₹8-15K per product. One-time cost per SKU. Worth it for a ₹30K+ piece; optional for a ₹3K piece.

04
Everywhere · Product cards

The card leans toward you.

Product cards throughout the site subtly tilt in 3D on hover — the arch parallaxes slightly deeper than the piece, so the piece appears to lean out of the frame toward the viewer. Small gesture, unforgettable feeling of closeness.

/collection · /homepage · cards
Concept · running
Copper Vastu Kalash
₹18,400

What it is

On cursor over the card, CSS 3D transform tilts the whole card 8° toward the cursor direction. The glyph inside translates 20px on Z, so it "pops out" of the arch. On mouseout, gently returns to rest.

Technical approach

Pure CSS. No library. perspective on parent, transform: rotateY() rotateX() on card via mouse position via one small event handler.

CSS 3DPointer events

Fallback

  • Touch devices — no hover; card gets a subtle lift on tap-and-hold instead.
  • Reduced motion — replace with a soft opacity fade.

Budget

Effectively zero. GPU-composited transform, no CPU cost. 60fps guaranteed.

The sun and moon, where they actually are.

A subtle astronomical strip inside the panchang shows the real current positions of Sun and Moon in the sky over the user's location. Sun traces its arc through the day; Moon rises and sets. Grounds the panchang in real astronomy, not just data — this is the source of Vedic calendar, made visible.

What it is

Live-computed sun altitude/azimuth + moon position for the user's lat/long and current time. Sun moves across its arc through the day (position updated every minute). Moon is where it actually is right now.

Technical approach

Astronomical calculations from Swiss Ephemeris (same source as our panchang). Positions computed server-side, rendered client-side as CSS transforms.

09
Home audit · Result

The compass needle settles.

At the end of the two-minute home audit, the recommended direction reveals as a compass needle physically settling to point that way — swinging past, coming back, oscillating, then locking into place. Feels earned, feels right, feels sacred.

/home-audit/result
Concept · running (loops)

What it is

The compass needle animates from -90° (west) past the target (60°) then oscillates back and forth (30°, 50°, 40°, 45°) until it settles on the recommended direction (45° = NE). Physically simulated damping.

Technical approach

CSS keyframe animation with a bezier easing curve that mimics rotational damping. Framer Motion for the recommended piece reveal after settling.

CSS keyframesFramer Motion

Fallback

  • Reduced motion — the needle appears already pointing to the direction.
  • No JS — the direction is stated in text without the compass visualization.

Budget

Effectively zero. Pure CSS, GPU-composited.

Technical stack

The libraries we use, and why.

Three.js~40KB (gzip)
3D scenes · WebGL

Industry standard. Used by Apple, Google, Nike, Rolex. Well-supported, huge ecosystem, GLB/GLTF loader for product models. Code-split per surface — only PDP + hero + kundali pages pay the cost.

React Three Fiber~15KB
3D · declarative

React wrapper for Three.js. Cleaner code, easier state management, better performance in React. What we'd use for the PDP product viewer and the mandala.

Framer Motion~30KB tree-shaken
Motion · orchestration

Used by Apple, Linear, Stripe. Scroll-triggered animations, sequencing, layout animations. The scroll-unfurl and chart-drawing moments.

GSAP~35KB
Complex sequences

For the specifically sequenced moments — writing effects, ink drawing, calligraphy character-by-character. Used when Framer Motion isn't enough. Only loaded on certificate + scroll-unfurl surfaces.

Lottie~20KB player
Hand-crafted micro-motion

For designer-authored moments — the diya lighting up, the bell ringing. Author in After Effects, ship as tiny JSON. Better than trying to CSS these.

Swiss Ephemerisserver-side
Astronomical data

Powers panchang + sun/moon positions + birth-chart planet positions. Server-computed, delivered as JSON — no client cost. The single source of truth.

CSS 3D + Canvas0KB
Everywhere possible

The card lean, the compass needle, the dome tilt, the candlelight glow — all pure CSS. Zero library cost. GPU-composited. This is the default; JavaScript is only added when the effect genuinely can't be CSS.

Intersection Observernative
Scroll-triggered reveals

Native browser API for detecting when elements enter viewport. Powers every scroll-triggered moment — no library needed. Standard, fast.

Performance discipline

Every 3D or heavy motion module is code-split. The homepage bundle doesn't include the PDP 360° viewer code, and the PDP doesn't include the kundali wheel code. Each moment loads only on the page that needs it. LCP under 2.5s is measured on every deploy — if we regress, the deploy fails.

Reference brands

Cartier heritage jewelry

Signature 3D jewelry rotating on product pages, everything else disciplined typography and quiet layouts. The exact one-signature-per-surface pattern we're using.

Rolex heritage watches

One scroll-triggered cinematic moment per collection page. Otherwise ruthlessly consistent grid and type. Proves signature moments amplify, not distract from, the system.

Apple product pages global tech

Every product page has ONE scroll-tied 3D moment. Buttons, tables, forms are boringly consistent. That's what makes the moment land.

Aesop craft skincare

Proof a devotional/artisan brand can feel breathtaking with almost NO 3D — just perfect typography, spacing, and one signature scroll moment per collection.

Bottega Veneta Winter seasonal fashion

Rolls-Royce configurator bespoke

Full 3D product viewer for a ₹6-crore purchase. Proof that 3D product viewing is table-stakes for high-value goods — same logic for our ₹30K+ pieces.

Linear product SaaS

Not devotional, but their scroll-triggered reveal discipline is the gold standard. Study their motion primitives — that's the level we set for our Framer Motion work.
