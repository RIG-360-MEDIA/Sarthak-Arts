Part two

What I studied, and what it changes.

You told me the current build was lifeless and thin — for a devotional Vastu brand carrying consultations, astrology, and social integration, that's true. I went back and did the research I should have done in the first place. Here's what I learned, and how it shapes the redesign above.

The bar

Reference brands worth aspiring to

Divine Hindu India · ₹100 Cr ARR in 1 year

The direct comparable. Trust as a feature — every product certified, premium pricing justified by that. Certified gemstones, Rudraksha, murtis, pooja kits. We match — with a heavier hand on craft, not commodity.

P·TAL India · UNESCO-listed Thatheras

Craft-first storytelling. Metal-typed shopping, artisan family history as the marketing. Ours: direction-first, but the same reverence for the maker's hand.

Kama Ayurveda India · luxury Ayurveda

The retail experience feels like "a sanctuary of ancient wellness." Digital equivalent: not a catalog — a space you enter, with expert-led consultations built into the shopping journey.

Forest Essentials India

"Immersive, multi-sensorial journey with every product." Every product page should feel earned — origin, hands, materials, ritual — not just specs.

Mejuri Global · handcraft jewelry

Awe from restraint — visual hierarchy and typography doing the work, not decoration. Minimalism that reads premium, not corporate.

Warby Parker & Glossier Global · quiz-led commerce

The quiz as digital concierge, not a form. Branching, conversational; ends with tailored recommendations and cross-sells. Our home-audit should be exactly this — not the flat quiz it is now.

Co·Star Global · astrology app

Proof that modern spiritual can be minimalist and mysterious — dark, high-contrast, poetic microcopy, personalized moments ("Ask the Void"). The opposite of the colorful zodiac tackiness of most spiritual sites.

Awwwards immersive brands e.g. Immersive Garden

The luxury bar in 2026 — cinematic hero, scroll-triggered narrative, tactile digitalism (360° views, immersive video). Restraint plus one great motion moment.

The audit

What was missing

Two categories. On the left: e-commerce essentials our current build simply doesn't have — table stakes for any premium D2C. On the right: the moves that were never there but are uniquely ours to make, because we're a direction-first devotional brand with consultations, astrology and social already in the platform.

Standard e-commerce essentials — missing

  • Wishlist (guest-friendly) — save without account
  • Quick view on card hover
  • Related & "complete the altar" cross-sell on PDP
  • Trust badges below "Add to cart" — payments, secure, returns
  • Photo reviews — 6× more influential than text-only
  • Sticky mobile Add-to-cart — +8–12% ATC on mobile
  • Multiple product images — currently one placeholder
  • Zoom on product images
  • 360° / video product views (Baymard "Tactile Digitalism")
  • Cart drawer, not a page navigation
  • Search autocomplete + suggested queries
  • Recently viewed strip
  • Product variants — size / finish / patina
  • Visual order tracking — timeline, not a status word
  • Customer accounts — currently guest-only
  • Gift wrap as an option (we have the gift note)
  • Loyalty / referral — a "Bhakta Circle"
  • Micro-interactions & considered empty states

Sarthak Arts–unique moves — never attempted

  • Live Choghadiya / Muhurta panel — the daily auspicious window shown in the header. No devotional D2C does this properly. It's a signature.
  • Direction wheel as a shrine, not a filter — each direction is a page: the element, the deity, the mantra, and the pieces built for it.
  • Ritual guidance on every PDP — Sankalpa → Cleansing → Placement → First Puja. Printable card ships in the box.
  • Certificate of composition as an heirloom — currently a plain PDF; should be an illuminated scroll worth framing.
  • Consultation woven through the journey — floating "Ask an astrologer / consultant" chip on PDP; post-purchase consecration reading upsell.
  • Birth-chart-linked recommendations — after one astrology session, product picks tune to the customer's chart.
  • Home-audit as a real concierge (Warby Parker–style branching), not a flat form.
  • "Bhaktas' homes" — a curated gallery of real customer altars with the pieces in place.
  • Live-from-the-workshop feed — occasional dispatches (not a static Instagram grid).
  • Ritual calendar — next festival, and the pieces relevant to it.
  • Deity-specific placement guidance on murti pages — currently generic.
  • Considered sound moments — a mantra plays on request, not autoplay (Hindu-specific UX).
The visual language

Parchment, ink, brass — with sanctuary interludes.

A warm, tactile default world for browsing; deep dark full-bleed panels for the sacred moments — the hero, the consultant, the certificate reveal. Boldness spent in one place, quietness everywhere else.

Palette

Parchment
Ink
Brass
Sanctuary

Parchment ground and ink for text — warm neutrals, chosen not defaulted. Brass carries every accent, from rules to headings. Sindoor red used rarely — for sacred moments only (a live puja indicator, a festival badge). Sanctuary charcoal is the "temple interior" dark panel.

Type

Made by hand.
A serif display face for headlines — Georgia works universally, no CDN gamble, ties visually to a printer's ledger. System sans for running body text — legible and disciplined. Uppercase eyebrows with generous letter-spacing. All numerals tabular, so weights and prices line up in columns like the ledger they belong to.

Motion & ritual

  • Stillness first. Motion is used as punctuation, not decoration. One quiet gesture per section.
  • Reveal on scroll — subtle fades, no springs, no bounces. Slow easing that respects the subject.
  • The mandala breathes. The hero glyph gently rotates over sixty seconds, once. It's the only ambient motion.
  • Sacred pauses. Full-bleed dark sections mark the moments that matter — the hero, the consultant, the certificate reveal.
  • Micro-generosity. Considered focus states, empty states with intention ("The altar is empty — begin here."), errors that instruct rather than apologize.
  • Reduced motion honored. Every animation collapses cleanly with prefers-reduced-motion.
The plan

How the rebuild unfolds.

Sign this off first — I don't touch code until you do. Then, in order:

01

Design tokens & type foundation

New palette, type scale, motion primitives, and a proper set of small components (button, chip, ledger row, ornament). Everything below is built from these.

02

Storefront — the customer world

Rebuild home, the direction shrine, PDP with ritual guidance and photo reviews, the "complete the altar" cross-sell, wishlist, cart drawer, sticky mobile ATC, quiz-as-concierge home audit, and the live Choghadiya panel.

03

The heirloom certificate

Redesign the certificate PDF as an illuminated document — deity, direction, exact metal weights, consecration date. Something worth framing.

04

Consultant + astrologer sanctuary

Rework the booking flow as an editorial moment, and add birth-chart-linked recommendations, "ask" chips on PDP, and a post-purchase consecration reading upsell.

05

Community, calendar, and social sanctuary

Bhaktas' homes gallery, ritual calendar with next festival + pieces, and a proper "live-from-the-workshop" feed instead of a static Instagram grid.

06

Admin & consultant interfaces, redesigned to the same bar

The dashboards you and the consultant sit in every day — same design language, warmth and rhythm carried through. Not utilitarian; not "admin gray."
